# About me

## A decade of studying the brain in health and disease

I'm Roman Baravalle, a neuroscientist specializing in clinical EEG. I work with recordings from real patients to measure how the brain changes in Parkinson's disease and cognitive decline.

For more than ten years I've studied how brain activity is organized, and how disease disrupts it: from single neurons and large-scale models of the cortex to EEG and intracranial recordings from patients. I've done this research at the Physics Institute of La Plata in Argentina, at LSU Health Sciences Center in New Orleans, and now at SUNY Downstate Health Sciences University.

What sets my work apart is the ability to move between two worlds that rarely meet: abstract mathematics and computer science on one side, and the complex, messy reality of biological data on the other. I can build a rigorous model or algorithm, then take it into real patient recordings, with all their noise, artifacts and exceptions, and make it work there. My PhD in physics (*summa cum laude*, National University of La Plata) gave me the mathematical foundation; years with clinical data taught me where theory meets reality.

## What I work on

Most of my work sits where neuroscience meets clinical questions:

- **Parkinson's disease and cognitive decline:** resting-state EEG markers tested across 500+ recordings from four independent datasets, and a biomarker developed for use with patients at a public hospital in La Plata. [Case study](/portfolio/parkinsons-eeg-biomarkers/) · [Code on GitHub](https://github.com/RomanBaravalle/Parkinson_rsEEG)
- **Sleep and drug effects:** measuring how a treatment changed sleep and brain activity across a 5-institution study. [*iScience*, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11986984)
- **Epilepsy:** working with clinicians at Hospital El Cruce to detect patterns in brain activity that come before seizures. [*Chaos*, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0101220)
- **Healthy ageing:** EEG signs linked to the risk of cognitive decline in older adults. [*Journal of Neuroscience Methods*, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2022.109608)

Alongside research, I've built tools used outside the lab. I was a backend developer on an AI assistant for therapists, and I've delivered consulting projects for research teams, including a validated model for an NIH grant proposal in six weeks.

## How I think about evidence

I care about three things:

- **Careful with data.** I check every step, and a finding counts only when it holds up in independent data.
- **Clear with people.** Results are only useful if the people who rely on them understand them. I've taught physics and data analysis for 11 years, and I write every analysis so it can be explained and handed over.
- **Honest about limits.** A good analysis says what the data can't tell you as clearly as what it can.

## Beyond the analysis

I serve on the Board of Directors of the Organization for Computational Neurosciences. I co-advise a PhD student, mentored for Google Summer of Code, and taught three cohorts at Neuromatch Academy. Through NSF I-Corps, I interviewed more than 25 people in pharma about what they need from brain data, and that shapes how I frame every project.

Outside work, I've practiced karate for 10 years. My favorite idea from it is *kyūdō mugen*, "the path of learning never ends." It's also how I approach every dataset.
